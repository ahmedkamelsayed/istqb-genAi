Deploying & Integrating Generative AI in Test Organizations

≈80 minutes · mostly recall & understand

5.1.1 (K1) Risks of Shadow AI

Shadow AI = use of unapproved/unsanctioned AI tools. Risks:

  • Information security & data privacy weaknesses — personal tools lack robust security → breaches.
  • Compliance & regulatory issues — unapproved tools → non-compliance → legal consequences.
  • Vague intellectual property — unclear licensing → IP disputes (esp. with copyrighted data).
A clear GenAI strategy/roadmap helps avoid shadow AI.

5.1.2 (K2) Key aspects of a GenAI strategy for testing

  • Define measurable test objectives — increase productivity, shorten cycles, improve quality.
  • Select the right LLMs aligned to objectives; compatible with existing test infrastructure/tools, scalable.
  • Data quality — accurate, relevant, secure input. High-quality data matters more than maximum data.
  • Training programs — technical and ethical skills.
  • Collect task-specific metrics (see 2.3.1).
  • Process guidelines — rules for sensitive data, transparency (label AI-generated content), quality gates reviewing generated testware.

5.1.3 (K2) Selecting LLMs/SLMs — the four key criteria

CriterionMeaning
Model performanceEvaluate against the organization’s own benchmarks for the target tasks (2.3.1 metrics).
Fine-tuning potentialCan/should it be fine-tuned with domain data for better accuracy?
Recurring costLicensing + operational/compute costs fit the budget. (A one-off PoC is non-recurring — distractor.)
Community & supportActive community + good documentation for implementation/troubleshooting.
Few benchmarks target software-test tasks specifically; also weigh functional capabilities (multimodal, reasoning), technical features (context window), licensing (commercial vs open source).

5.1.4 (K1) Three phases of adopting GenAI

  1. Discovery — awareness & capability building: train teams, provide LLM access, experiment with initial use cases (build confidence).
  2. Initiation and usage definition — identify & prioritize practical use cases; evaluate infrastructure; develop expertise; align with org needs.
  3. Utilization and iteration — full integration; continuous monitoring, measurement, and management for sustainable, scalable benefits.
Phases can run in parallel for different use cases. Address early concerns (e.g., fear of job displacement).

5.2.1 (K2) Essential skills & knowledge for testers

  • Master prompt engineering; understand context windows; develop test review methods.
  • Combine domain + test expertise with AI skills to evaluate LLM-driven testing.
  • Key competencies: assess LLM capabilities, prompt-refinement, evaluate AI-generated testware.
  • Know GenAI risks + mitigations; understand data-security implications of sharing testware; apply data sanitization + privacy-preserving prompting.
  • Environmental awareness — right-size models, optimize usage, balance automation vs cost/energy.
❌ Testers can’t prevent hallucinations (intrinsic) — they identify & mitigate. Building the LLM is an AI-developer skill, not a tester skill.

5.2.2 (K1) Building GenAI capabilities in teams

  • Hands-on, gradual learning: guided exercises, peer learning, knowledge sharing — not a “big-bang” rollout or purely theoretical courses.
  • Progress from basic prompt creation → focused, test-specific techniques.
  • Prompt pattern = a reusable template for effective prompts → consistent, reliable output.
  • Communities of practice share prompt-pattern libraries and lessons learned → continuous improvement.

5.2.3 (K1) Evolving roles & responsibilities

RoleShifts to…
TestersAI-assisted test specialists — combine test techniques with guiding & verifying AI-generated testware, prompt refinement, maintaining test-specific prompt libraries.
Test managersAI-based test strategy, AI-based risk management, monitoring/controlling AI-based processes, AI governance, balancing human + AI, and coordinating hybrid teams of people + GenAI agents.
Managers keep their management focus (not deep model internals); testers, not managers, refine prompts; neither relies solely on AI.
